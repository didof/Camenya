# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Camenya serves one person recording and shaping a direct-to-camera story on an iPhone. During capture the user is watching framing, recalling a script, and may alternate between front and rear cameras inside one Take. During review the same person needs a small, dependable editing grammar that removes mistakes and arranges the useful moments without exposing the complexity of a general video editor.

## Product Purpose

Camenya is a narrative recorder with a non-destructive Primary Storyline. It lets the user record immutable Takes, then select, trim, split, reorder, remove, and mute the Timeline Clips needed to construct a story. Only an explicit Project Export produces the finished movie that may be saved to Photos.

Camenya is not a multitrack video editor or creator suite. Capture stays primary, editing stays bounded, and recoverable source media remains understandable.

## Brand Personality

Calm, tactile, trustworthy. Camenya should feel focused and native, with the confidence of a dedicated physical recording tool and none of the noise of a creator platform.

## Anti-references

- A professional camera control deck packed with exposure, lens, waveform, and codec controls.
- A social-video creator interface filled with filters, stickers, feeds, trends, and promotional prompts.
- A miniature desktop editor with arbitrary tracks, transition catalogs, keyframes, and dense persistent panels.
- A decorative glass dashboard whose effects compete with the live preview.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the active task primary: the live preview while capturing, and the current frame and selected Clip while editing.
2. Make the next valid action obvious. Recorder actions must not compete equally, and Timeline tools appear only when their target and effect are clear.
3. Show truth plainly. Recording state, playhead position, selected range, removed content, pending work, and export state must be unmistakable.
4. Preserve intent non-destructively. Editing changes Storyline metadata, not the immutable Take source, and recovery is explicit rather than magical.
5. Earn every control. If it does not support capture, the bounded Storyline grammar, caption review, or Project Export, it does not belong.
6. Prefer familiar iPhone behavior. Use native navigation, controls, typography, accessibility, feedback, and system conventions unless a media-specific control genuinely needs a custom surface.
7. Protect confidence. Destructive actions, interruptions, recovery, asynchronous work, and saving must explain what is safe.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use native Dynamic Type, VoiceOver labels and hints, minimum 44-point targets, strong contrast over arbitrary video imagery, and semantic state that does not rely on color alone. Every gesture has a visible, named alternative. Motion communicates state in 150 to 250 milliseconds and respects Reduce Motion through native system behavior.
